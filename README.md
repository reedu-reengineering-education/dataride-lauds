# LAUDS - DATA-RIDE
DATA-RIDE resources from the LAUDS Factories programme in Nancy: Campaign maps, policy recommendations, data-processing methodology, Visual toolkit, posters, guides, graphical invitations and project photos. Together, they document citizen-generated cycling data, collective learning and advocacy for better cycling conditions.

# HOW TO USE
This guide brings together the technical resources and advocacy materials from the DATA-RIDE campaign in Nancy. Part 1 covers how to set up the senseBox:bike with two lateral distance sensors and process the data. Part 2 explains how to use cycling data and community knowledge to support local action.

## Part 1 — Technology: Assembly, setup and data processing

1. **Prepare the enclosure.** Download the files for the senseBox:bike enclosure. [Enclosure files](https://cloud.reedu.de/s/xwMSJnCnys7NBzR)

2. **Assemble the hardware.** Check the list of components and tools, then follow the assembly and wiring instructions. [Assembly guide](DATA-RIDE%20-%20Assembly%20guide.pdf)

3. **Upload the firmware.** Use the version adapted for the two lateral distance sensors used in Nancy. [Nancy firmware](https://github.com/sensebox/senseBox-bike-Arduino/tree/main/senseBox-bike-nancy)

4. **Mount the device.** For a rear-rack installation, consult the guide for the prototype developed with support from Université de Lorraine and the Lorraine Fab Living Lab in Nancy. [Alternative mounting support guide](DATA-RIDE%20-%20Alternative%20Mounting%20Support.pdf)

5. **Set up the app.** Follow the installation and configuration instructions in the app repository. [senseBox:bike app](https://github.com/reedu-reengineering-education/sensebox-bike-flutter)

6. **Collect and process data.** Record rides with the device and app, then follow the methodology to download and clean the data and detect potential overtaking events. Configure the required device identifiers locally before running the scripts. [Processing methodology](DATA_RIDE%20-%20Processing_methodology.pdf) · [Data and processing scripts](Data%20and%20Processing)

## Part 2 — Advocacy: From information to action

Local organisations, cycling advocates and community groups can adapt the DATA-RIDE findings and recommendations to their own priorities, resources and context.

1. **Identify local priorities.** Combine existing research and mobility data with cyclists’ everyday experiences to identify shared concerns and decide where to focus.

2. **Build a local coalition.** Bring together cyclists, residents, associations, researchers and other local partners. Discuss the findings and agree on common objectives while making room for different perspectives and needs.

3. **Turn findings into accessible messages.** Select a few findings and explain what they show and where the evidence is limited. Adapt the project’s materials for posters, social media or public discussions. [DATA-RIDE Visual Toolkit](DATA-RIDE%20-%20Toolkit.pdf)

4. **Engage with decision-makers.** Use the findings to start conversations with local authorities and mobility stakeholders. Adapt proposals to local policies and planning processes, and agree on concrete next steps where possible. [DATA-RIDE Policy Recommendations](DATA-RIDE%20-%20Policy%20Recommendation.pdf)

5. **Keep the process going.** Share the outcomes, invite feedback and revisit priorities as new information emerges. Where possible, organise follow-up data collection or bring people together to interpret the results.

### Adapting DATA-RIDE to another city

Use the Visual Toolkit to choose activities that fit your local resources and priorities, from mapping local partners and setting up sensors to interpreting results and planning advocacy. Adapt the sequence to your context while keeping the link between citizen-generated evidence, collective learning and local action.
